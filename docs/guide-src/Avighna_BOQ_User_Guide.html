1What the application does

Avighna BOQ turns a construction drawing into a quantity take-off. A site engineer uploads a drawing (DWG preferred; DXF, PDF and images also work). The system reads the rooms, doors, windows, walls, slabs, columns and beams from it, measures every item the way a quantity surveyor would, and produces an Excel file with three linked results:

1. Drop the drawingDWG / DXF / PDF / image. One floor or area per job.
→
2. AI + engine read itRooms, openings, walls, slabs, members, other items — with sizes, finishes and evidence.
→
3. Measurement SheetItem-wise blocks, one row per drawing object, Nos × L × B × H, deductions as minus rows (IS 1200).
→
4. BOQ abstractOne line per item with description, quantity and unit. Rates are left blank.
→
5. Material requirementCement bags, sand, tiles, paint, steel… = quantity × norm × wastage.

Everything that comes out of the system is traceable. Every row of the measurement sheet points to a drawing object (a wall, a door, a room). Every element the AI read carries a confidence score and an evidence note. Every quantity in the BOQ is a live Excel formula that links back to the measurement sheet. Reviewers can correct any element in the browser and recompute; the Excel is produced only from the corrected data.

What it is not

  • It is not a pricing tool. Rates and amounts are intentionally blank in the BOQ; your estimator fills them in Excel.
  • It is not a replacement for a bar bending schedule. Reinforcement steel is a thumb-rule estimate (kg per cum) and is flagged as such.
  • It does not guess silently. When the drawing does not give a size, the element is skipped or marked low-confidence and a warning is shown.
Where to find it. Web app: https://avighna-boq.vercel.app  ·  API: https://avighna-boq.onrender.com  ·  On the development Mac: http://localhost:3000. Sign in with the account your administrator created for you.

3Key terms, explained simply

JobOne upload = one job. A job holds the drawing files, the sheets read from them, the elements, the measurement result and the Excel. Name it by project and floor (e.g. "Nishika Elysium — 6th floor").
SheetOne page of a PDF, or one layout of a DWG/DXF (usually the Model space). Each sheet is classified (floor plan, finishes plan, door schedule, elevation, …) and gets a preview image.
ElementsWhat was read from the drawing, in six groups: Rooms (name, L × W × H, area, finishes), Openings (doors, windows, ventilators with W × H and count), Walls (RCC / block / partition with L, thickness, H), Slabs, Columns & beams, and Other items (anything countable: furniture, fixtures, counters, thresholds).
AssumptionsProject-level defaults used when a value is not on the drawing: floor height, wall thickness, slab thickness, construction type (Mivan / conventional), concrete grade. Editable on the Elements tab.
ConfidenceHow sure the reader is about an element, from 0 to 100 %. Anything the AI had to infer (e.g. a height not on the drawing) is marked low; anything you edit becomes 100 %.
EvidenceA short note of where the value came from: the text on the drawing, the dimension line, the schedule row, or "edited by …".
Room-wiseThe same quantities regrouped per room: each room's items, openings, other items and materials, plus a common section for whole-floor items.
Measurement SheetThe detailed take-off. Grouped by item (e.g. "12 mm internal plaster"). Each row is one object from the drawing with No. × L × B × H = Qty. Deductions (doors, windows) appear as minus rows. Each block ends with a total.
BOQ (Abstract)The summary: one line per item with a CPWD-style description, the total quantity (linked to the measurement sheet), the unit, and blank rate / amount columns.
Material RequirementFor every BOQ line, the materials needed: BOQ quantity × consumption norm × (1 + wastage %). Summed per material at the bottom (e.g. total cement bags).
BasisHow a BOQ line was obtained: measured from drawing objects, thumb rule (steel kg/cum — replace with BBS), or manual.
StatusWhere a job is in its life: Queued → Converting → Analysing → Extracting → Quantifying → Ready for review → Approved. Failed if something went wrong (the message says what).

4Quick start in 6 steps

  1. Sign in at the web address with your e-mail and password. On first sign-in you set your own password.
  2. Click New measurement. Enter the project name and the floor / level. Drop the drawing files (DWG preferred). Click Upload & measure.
  3. Wait for the status to reach Ready for review (typically 2–5 minutes per sheet; large DWGs take longer). The pipeline panel on the right shows each step.
  4. Open the Elements tab. Check the rooms, openings and walls against the drawing. Fix anything wrong, add what is missing, then click Save & recompute.
  5. Read the Measurement Sheet, BOQ and Materials tabs. Warnings at the top tell you what to double-check.
  6. A reviewer clicks Approve. Anyone clicks Download Excel to get the workbook with live formulas.

6Step 2 — Create a new measurement

Figure 3 — New measurement: project details and the drawing drop zone.
Project name requiredThe project the drawing belongs to. Appears in the Excel title and the job list.
Floor / Level requiredWhich floor or area the drawing shows, e.g. "6th floor", "Sales centre", "Typical floor 6–17". Used in every measurement-sheet row and lets you compare floors later.
Building / WingOptional: tower or wing name.
Drawing filesDrop one or more files: DWG (best — exact vector text and geometry), DXF, PDF (vector plots are read exactly; scanned PDFs are read from the image), PNG / JPG / TIFF. Up to 200 MB per upload.

Which files to upload together

  • Put one floor (or one area) in one job. Upload the plan plus its supporting sheets: the floor finishes plan, the door / window schedule, and section or detail sheets that carry heights. The reader merges them — a door schedule gives sizes and counts, the plan gives which room each door is in.
  • Do not mix two different floors in one job; create a job per floor instead (the Excel has a per-floor "Tender vs Site" template).
  • Xref base plans are useful but not required; the sheet that carries the room names and dimensions matters most.

Click Upload & measure. The browser shows the upload progress and then opens the job page.

Drawing preview

Above the Pipeline panel the Drawing preview card shows the rendered sheet of the uploaded drawing (the first sheet, with a thumbnail strip when there are several). Click the image to open it full-screen in high resolution: zoom with the mouse wheel or the + / − buttons, drag to pan, Fit shows the whole sheet, 100% shows it pixel for pixel, and Download saves the PNG. Use it to check the drawing against the elements without opening AutoCAD. Jobs processed before this feature show the standard-resolution image until they are reprocessed.

Figure 4 — The drawing preview opened full-screen, zoomed into the plan.
Messages you may see in the pipeline panel. "preview could not be rendered" — the drawing was too heavy to draw within the time limit; the AI still reads the text and CAD facts. "AI reading failed … rooms taken from CAD outlines only" — the AI service did not answer; the fallback reads room names and door tags from the CAD file and shows rooms as equivalent squares at 50 % confidence — check them. "Room X: size missing, skipped" — no dimensions were found for that room; add them on the Elements tab.

8Step 4 — Check and correct the Elements

This is the most important screen. The measurement sheet, BOQ and materials are all computed from what is on this tab, so five minutes here decide the quality of the whole output.

Figure 5 — Elements tab: Assumptions card, then Rooms, Openings, Walls, Slabs, Columns & beams and Other items. Each row shows its confidence; the "i" opens the evidence.

What to check, in order

  1. Assumptions — floor height, wall thickness, slab thickness, construction type, concrete grade. These fill every blank on the drawing, so set them first.
  2. Rooms — every room on the plan is listed once, with L × W (or a measured area), height, and the floor / wall / ceiling finish. Mark wet areas (toilets, pantry) so waterproofing and dado are measured. Skirting height and dado height are per room.
  3. Openings — each door / window type with width, height, count and the room it belongs to. Counts come from the schedule; the room name is used to deduct the opening from that room's plaster, paint and skirting.
  4. Walls, Slabs, Columns & beams — structural items, if the drawing shows them. Leave empty for a pure finishes job.
  5. Other items — countable things the AI noticed (reception desk, workstations, counters, thresholds). They go to the BOQ as "nos" / "LS" lines.

Editing

  • Click a row (or its pencil) to open the edit dialog. Numbers must be positive; counts must be at least 1.
  • Add creates a new row in that section; Delete removes one (you are asked to confirm).
  • Edited rows get confidence 100 % and an evidence note "edited by <you> on <date>".
  • Nothing is saved until you press Save & recompute. The button is grey until something changed. Discard throws the changes away.
  • Saving sends the whole element list to the server, recomputes every tab and refreshes the Excel on the next download.
Figure 6 — Editing a room. Finishes drive flooring, skirting, plaster and paint items; "wet area" adds waterproofing and dado.
Rule of thumb. If a value is on the drawing and the app got it wrong, correct it here — never in Excel. The Excel is regenerated from these elements, and a corrected element keeps the audit trail (who changed what, when).

10Step 6 — BOQ, Materials, Room-wise and Sheets tabs

Figure 9 — BOQ tab: one line per item, grouped by trade, with a basis badge. Quantities only; rates are left blank on purpose.

BOQ — the abstract. Item number, CPWD-style description, quantity, unit and basis. measured means the quantity comes from measured drawing objects; thumb rule is used only for reinforcement steel and must be replaced by a bar bending schedule before tender.

Figure 10 — Materials tab: for each BOQ line the material, norm, wastage and required quantity; a summary by material at the end.

Materials — for each BOQ line, the materials it consumes. Required = BOQ qty × norm × (1 + wastage %). The norms are company standards (Appendix B); an admin can tune them. The summary table at the bottom adds up each material across all items (e.g. total cement bags for plaster + flooring + masonry).

Figure 11 — Room-wise tab: one card per room with its work items, doors / windows and other items, and the materials for that room; a "Common / whole floor" card holds what is not tied to one room.

Room-wise — the same quantities regrouped by room, for clients and site teams who want to know what goes into each room. Pick a room from the selector or view all. Each card shows the room's size, area, perimeter and finishes, then three tables: Work items (this room's share of every BOQ item, e.g. its flooring, skirting, plaster, paint, dado, waterproofing), Doors, windows & other items (with the count that falls in this room — a door type shared by several rooms is split between them), and Materials for this room (cement, sand, tiles, paint … computed from this room's items) with a summary by material. RCC walls, slabs, columns and beams, and anything marked "various" go to the Common / whole floor card at the end. Rooms plus common always add up to the Measurement Sheet totals.

Figure 12 — Sheets tab: every sheet read from the files with its preview, classification and processing notes.

Sheets — what the system saw: each file / page / layout, its preview image, the detected sheet type (floor plan, finishes plan, door schedule, elevation, …), units, drawing number, and processing notes. Use it to confirm that the right sheets were read and that previews rendered.

13How quantities are calculated (rules)

The engine follows IS 1200 (method of measurement of building works) and common Indian QS practice. The rules below are what the measurement sheet applies; every row names the rule it used.

ItemRule
Flooring (per finish)Room area (L × W, or the measured area if the drawing gives one). One BOQ item per finish type (e.g. "FC-201 1200×1200 stone").
SkirtingRoom perimeter minus the widths of the doors in that room (100 mm high by default, per room).
Internal plasterPerimeter × height for plastered rooms. Openings: ≤ 0.5 sqm — no deduction; 0.5 to 3 sqm — deduct one face; > 3 sqm — deduct both faces and add jambs and soffits.
Ceiling plaster / paintRoom area.
Wall paintSame area as internal plaster, same opening rule; the paint system comes from the room's wall finish.
Dado (wall tiles)Wet areas only: perimeter × dado height, minus door widths × dado height.
WaterproofingWet areas only: floor area + 300 mm upturn along the perimeter.
Doors / windowsCounted in nos from the openings list (one item per tag, size and description in the text). Windows also give an area for fabricated items.
RCC wallsNos × L × thickness × H minus openings (Nos × W × thickness × H).
Formwork to wallsContact area, both faces: 2 × L × H minus 2 × opening area.
ColumnsConcrete W × D × storey height; formwork = perimeter × height (4 sides).
BeamsConcrete W × (D − slab thickness) × L (the part below the slab); formwork = 2 sides + soffit.
SlabsConcrete L × W × thickness; formwork = soffit minus beam widths.
Block masonrycum for walls ≥ 150 mm (L × thickness × H minus openings); sqm for thinner partitions.
Reinforcement steelThumb rule, flagged in the BOQ: walls 125, columns 200, beams 150, slabs 85 kg per cum. Replace with the bar bending schedule for tender.
Other itemsTaken as given (nos / rmt / sqm / LS) and placed under their trade.

Rounding: dimensions are kept to 3 decimals; quantities are rounded to 3 decimals in the app and left as formulas in Excel.

14How the AI reading works and its limits

The AI (Google Gemini 2.5 Pro) does not estimate areas from a picture. For each sheet it receives three things: the preview image (for layout), the exact vector text of the drawing with coordinates (so numbers are never misread), and measured CAD facts computed by the engine from the DXF — room outlines with area and perimeter, hatch areas with their finish codes, door / window tag counts, polyline lengths. It returns the elements in a strict structure with confidence and evidence. The engine then does all the arithmetic.

What it is good at

  • Floor plans with room names and dimension lines; finishes plans with finish tags (FC-201 …); door / window schedules with sizes and counts; general notes with heights and construction type.
  • Merging several sheets: the plan gives rooms, the schedule gives sizes, the finishes plan gives materials.

Where to be careful

  • Elevation and section sheets are not plans: the small coded hatches on them are legend swatches, not areas. Upload them for heights, but check any room that appears only from an elevation.
  • Furniture / general arrangement plans without dimensions give room names but no sizes. Add the finishes plan or the dimensioned plan in the same job.
  • Scanned PDFs and photos have no vector text, so numbers are read from the image — expect lower confidence.
  • Very large DWGs (20 MB and more) may not render a preview within the time limit; the AI then reads from text and CAD facts only, and the pipeline says so.
  • When the AI service is unavailable, the fallback produces rooms as equivalent squares at 50 % confidence — use it only as a starting point.
Always check low-confidence rows. The Elements tab counts them in the alert at the top. A value below 70 % means the reader inferred it; confirm it against the drawing.

15Preparing drawings for best results

  • Export the DWG in AutoCAD 2018 format (AC1032) with the plan in Model space. Purge unused blocks to reduce size.
  • Name every room with text inside its outline; keep dimension lines on the plan (overall and room-wise).
  • Use door / window tags (D1, W2, …) on the plan and include the schedule sheet with width × height and counts.
  • Tag finishes with codes (FC-201 stone, FC-202 carpet …) pointing into the hatched area via leaders; one hatch per zone.
  • State heights (floor-to-floor, false ceiling, skirting, dado) in notes or sections — otherwise the Assumptions defaults are used.
  • One floor per job. For typical floors that repeat, measure one floor and multiply in the BOQ, or create one job per floor to catch differences.
  • PDF: prefer the vector plot from AutoCAD over a scan; keep text as text (do not outline fonts).

Users

  • Invite a user with name, e-mail and role. A temporary password is shown once — pass it on securely. The user must change it at first sign-in.
  • Change role at any time (engineer / reviewer / admin). The change applies on the user's next request.

  • The first admin account is created from the server's BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_EMAIL / BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_PASSWORD settings on first start.

System card and audit log

  • The System card shows the API version, whether AI reading is on and which models are used, which DWG converter is available, database and storage backends, memory limits and worker threads.
  • The Audit log lists sign-ins, element edits, approvals, send-backs, user changes and settings changes with user and time.
  • Backup: admins can download a database backup from the API (GET /api/v1/admin/backup).

Standards and norms

Material consumption norms and wastage percentages (Appendix B) and the steel thumb rules are company standards kept on the server. Change them through the Standards API (admin) and reprocess or recompute jobs to apply them; the Excel shows which norm was used on every material row.

17Architecture, hosting and local setup

Web app (frontend)Next.js 15 / React 19, hosted on Vercel (avighna-boq.vercel.app). Talks to the API over HTTPS with a signed-in token.
API (backend)Python FastAPI, hosted on Render as a Docker container (avighna-boq.onrender.com). Contains the DWG converter (LibreDWG 0.13.4), the sheet readers, the AI wrapper, the measurement engine and the Excel writer. Two worker threads process jobs in the background.
Database and filesSupabase: PostgreSQL for users, jobs, sheets, elements, results and the audit log (row-level security enabled); Supabase Storage for uploaded drawings, previews and Excel files.
AIGoogle Gemini 2.5 Pro (reading) and 2.5 Flash (classification), called from the API only.
DeploymentEvery push to the main branch of github.com/ajs-avg/Avighna-BOQ redeploys Vercel (about 1 minute) and Render (about 10 minutes, because the DWG converter is built from source).

18Troubleshooting and FAQ

SymptomCause and what to do
"Cannot reach the API … Is the backend running?" on the sign-in pageThe API is down or waking up (Render free instances sleep after idle and take about a minute to wake). Wait and retry; on a laptop start the backend.
Banner "AI reading is off"No GEMINI_API_KEY on the server. Rooms come from CAD outlines only. Ask the admin to set the key, then Reprocess.
Job finished in seconds with strange rooms ("Entrance 44 × 44 m")The AI did not run (no key, service down, or no readable sheet). Check the pipeline messages and the Sheets tab; reprocess once the service is back.
"preview could not be rendered"Drawing too heavy to draw in time. Reading continues from text and CAD facts. Purge the DWG or upload the PDF plot as well.
"DWG conversion failed for …"The file is not a DWG, is password-protected, or uses a format the converter cannot read. Save as AutoCAD 2018 DWG or DXF and upload again.
Job Failed: "No sheets could be read"None of the files produced a readable sheet (empty PDF, image-only PDF with no content, corrupt file). Check the files and re-upload.
"Room X: size missing, skipped"No dimensions found for that room. Add L × W on the Elements tab or include the dimensioned plan.
"Door D5: room 'RECEPTION' not found …"The door schedule names a room that is not in the Rooms list, so no deduction was made. Add the room or correct the door's room field.

Approve is disabledOnly reviewers and admins can approve, only jobs in Ready for review, and not while edits are unsaved.

FAQ

Can I measure a PDF without the DWG? Yes. A vector PDF (plotted from CAD) keeps exact text and is read well; a scanned PDF is read from the image with lower confidence.

Does the app price the BOQ? No. Rates are blank by design; the estimator fills them in Excel and the amount formulas take over.

Where does steel come from? From thumb rules (kg per cum of concrete), clearly marked. Use the bar bending schedule for tender quantities.

Can I change the consumption norms? Yes, an admin can update them on the server (Standards). The Excel shows the norm used on every row.

Is my drawing sent anywhere? The drawing is stored in the company's Supabase storage and the sheet image plus text is sent to Google's Gemini API for reading. No other third party is involved.

22Appendix D — Glossary

BOQBill of Quantities — the list of work items with quantities (and, when priced, rates and amounts).
Measurement sheetThe detailed working that shows how each BOQ quantity was derived, object by object.
IS 1200Indian Standard "Method of measurement of building and civil engineering works"; defines what to measure, in which unit, and which deductions to make.
CPWD descriptionThe standard wording of work items used by the Central Public Works Department (e.g. "Providing and laying …").
DeductionA minus row that removes the area or volume of an opening or overlap from an item.
Contact areaFormwork is measured by the area of shuttering in contact with concrete.
Thumb ruleAn approximate ratio used when detailed data is not available (e.g. kg of steel per cum of concrete).
NormStandard consumption of a material per unit of work (e.g. 0.105 bags of cement per sqm of 12 mm plaster).
WastageExtra material allowed for cutting, breakage and handling, as a percentage.
DWG / DXFAutoCAD drawing formats; DXF is the open text version the readers use.
Model space / layoutIn CAD, the model space holds the real-size drawing; layouts are the plotted sheets.
HatchA filled pattern in CAD that marks an area (e.g. a floor finish zone).
MivanAluminium-formwork construction where walls and slabs are cast in RCC monolithically.
Tender vs SiteComparison of the quantities estimated at tender with those measured on site during execution.
